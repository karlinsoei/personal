Everything I'm good at, I was bad at first.

Karlin Oei — Houston, Texas

the story

I was the kid who played too much.

For years that was the whole conversation. Too much.

Then I found people who asked what rank I was, not how many hours.

Then a team. Which is a different thing from friends who play.

A team notices when you don't show up.

We competed. It turned into roughly $80,000 in esports scholarships.

The scholarships weren't the thing.

The thing was that someone finally treated what I loved as worth being good at.

That's what changed. Not the game.

what I'm building

EKUZO is me trying to build on purpose the thing that happened to me by luck.

Youth esports, coached properly — the part where someone takes it seriously, made repeatable instead of left to chance.

outside of work

I never stopped doing the thing — picking something I'm embarrassingly bad at, then staying long enough to get good.

Backflips. Landing maybe one in four. The other three are also on video.

Bouldering. Bad at the problems that need patience, which is most of them.

Piano first, then cello — started late enough that it stayed humbling.

Honda S2000, AP2. Learning to actually drive it is its own long project.

Still playing League. Still reviewing my own replays like film study.

Houston. Home, and where the kids we coach actually are.

It's the same pattern every time: be bad at it in public, find the people who take it seriously, stay.

If you're building something in this direction too, I'd genuinely like to hear about it.
