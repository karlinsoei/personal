Hi, I'm Karlin. I'm the kid who played too much.

Houston, Texas

where I came from

Childhood was hard. Social anxiety, and parents who split up.

League of Legends and Call of Duty became my refuge — and, it turned out, my way out.

Not a metaphor. It was where I actually talked to people.

Then in college I found five guys who loved it as much as I did.

  2. Keen

We competed. We won $80,000 in scholarships.

For gaming.

The thing I'd been told to stop doing was finally treated as worth being good at.

what I've been doing since

So I spent six years trying to understand why that mattered.

What happens when you take a kid's passion for games seriously?

What that's looked like

  • Building gaming and competitive esports programs with schools, families, and community organizations.
  • Designing curriculum and learning experiences around games, together with coaches.
  • Organizing teams, leagues, competitions, camps, and events.
  • Creating structured online spaces built around social skills and development.

What I've come to believe

  • The impact doesn't stop at the kid. Parents, schools, and coaches need it just as much.
  • Kids work incredibly hard when they care about the outcome and are given real ownership.
  • My story isn't unique. The magic can be replicated.
